## 1. CONTEXT

Human Energy Economics (HEE) developed from a central practical observation: organizations can lose execution capacity not only because they lack people, skills, technology, or resources, but because existing organizational capacity is repeatedly consumed by unnecessary, preventable, poorly designed, or unnecessarily human-mediated work.

During the author's tenure as Chief Innovation Officer (CInO) at **Abul Khair Group (AKG)**, a diversified Bangladeshi business group, a number of technology and operating interventions were designed and implemented to address recurring operational demand, internal capability, external dependency, process design, prevention, self-service, and organizational enablement.

**Official website:** [Abul Khair Group](https://www.abulkhairgroup.com/)

These interventions were not originally designed as demonstrations of HEE. They were practical responses to operating problems.

Over time, however, repeated patterns became visible.

The practical question increasingly shifted from:

> **How can we make existing work faster?**

to:

> **Why does this work continue to consume organizational capacity at all?**

This shift produced a different intervention logic.

Instead of only processing demand more efficiently, interventions increasingly sought to:

- remove unnecessary recurring demand;
- reduce avoidable activity;
- replace human-mediated work with controlled alternatives;
- re-engineer recurring processes and operating conditions;
- prevent recurring problems rather than repeatedly respond to them;
- develop and enable internal capability;
- redesign predictable demand;
- reduce unnecessary external dependency; and
- convert capability into effective application.

The central practical idea became simple:

> **The drain is stopped.**

The preservation of organizational capacity is the consequence.

## 2. WHY THESE CASES ARE PRESENTED

The cases in this record are selected from a broader body of operating work undertaken at AKG.

They are presented not merely as technology success stories, process improvements, or individual achievements, but as author-reported operating observations that illustrate how recurring organizational drain can be identified and addressed.

The cases illustrate different forms of organizational consumption:

- recurring support demand;
- unnecessary human-mediated service chains;
- preventable incidents;
- inefficient information production;
- capability constrained by organizational conditions;
- external dependency; and
- predictable demand processed through unnecessarily repetitive mechanisms.

The central question across the cases is:

> **What organizational capacity is being consumed, why is it being consumed, and does it need to be consumed in this way?**

The resulting practical logic can be expressed as:

**IDENTIFY DRAIN → DIAGNOSE CONDITION → SELECT INTERVENTION → STOP / REDUCE DRAIN → PRESERVE CAPACITY → ENABLE APPLICATION → STRENGTHEN EXECUTION**

This became an important practical pathway through which HEE evolved.

## 3. CENTRAL HEE PRACTICAL LOGIC

HEE examines not only organizational output, but also the organizational capacity consumed in producing that output.

A recurring operational pattern can be represented as:

**Recurring Operational Demand → Human Energy Consumption → Available Capacity Reduced → Higher-Value Application Constrained**

The reverse logic is:

**Identify Drain → Diagnose Condition → Select Intervention → Stop / Reduce / Replace / Re-engineer / Retain → Preserve Capacity → Enable Application → Strengthen Execution**

This distinction is important.

> **Stopping the drain is not the same as improving the response to the drain.**

An organization may become highly efficient at processing unnecessary demand while continuing to consume Human Energy and organizational capacity on that demand.

HEE therefore asks a prior question:

> **Should the organization still be consuming this capacity in this way?**

This record applies a drain lens. The Core README adds two separate checks: whether capacity spent was **Justified?** and whether next-cycle demand is **Covered?** The cases below mainly address the first: they judge a recurring demand not warranted in its current form (Case 6 judges the demand legitimate). They do not assess whether future capacity was covered, so that question is **Not assessed** here.

# 4. CASE 1 — PERFORMANCE WIZARD

## 4.1 Operating Condition

Users experienced recurring application login-performance problems, desktop slowness, and Windows-related performance issues.

The resulting support demand generated approximately **15 support requests per day across the relevant problem category**.

The conventional response would have been to continue receiving, diagnosing, and resolving these requests.

The intervention instead questioned whether the recurring demand could be addressed differently.

## 4.2 Intervention

A self-service **Performance Wizard** was developed.

The objective was to enable users to perform controlled performance-related actions themselves rather than repeatedly depend on IT personnel for routine intervention.

The primary 5R logic was:

> **REPLACE**

Human-mediated recurring support was replaced by a controlled self-service mechanism.

## 4.3 Observed Result

The recurring requests addressed by the wizard were removed from the normal IT support workload.

The intervention therefore did not simply improve the speed of request handling.

It changed the demand structure itself.

## 4.4 HEE Interpretation

The case illustrates a central HEE principle:

> **Do not optimize the handling of unnecessary recurring demand before asking whether the demand can be removed or replaced.**

The effect sought is not merely fewer tickets.

Reduced support work may create an opportunity to release Human Energy and organizational capacity previously consumed by repetitive support activity. Whether it did depends on how the time was reallocated, any displaced workload, maintenance costs, and subsequent use. This record does not document any of these (see Evidence recorded).

# 5. CASE 2 — RDP / COMPUTER RESTART SELF-SERVICE

## 5.1 Operating Condition

Computer restart requirements generated a recurring service chain:

**User → Local IT → Central IT → Schedule → Action → Notification**

Approximately **20 restart calls per day across the relevant demand category** were associated with this recurring requirement.

The technical action itself was simple.

The organizational service chain was not.

Human Energy was being consumed by coordination, scheduling, intervention, and notification around a task that could be controlled differently.

## 5.2 Intervention

A self-service restart wizard was introduced.

Users could initiate the required restart through a controlled mechanism without requiring the full support chain.

The primary 5R logic was:

> **REPLACE**

## 5.3 Observed Result

The recurring restart calls were reported to have been reduced to **zero following the intervention** (author-reported; the observation period and records are Not recorded).

The important change was therefore not simply faster restart execution.

The recurring human-mediated service chain was removed.

## 5.4 HEE Interpretation

This case illustrates that organizational drain can arise from the **service structure surrounding a task**, rather than from the technical complexity of the task itself.

The practical question became:

> **Why should a simple recurring technical action continue to require a multi-step human service chain?**

# 6. CASE 3 — QUARTERLY HARDWARE HEALTH CHECK

## 6.1 Operating Condition

Recurring hardware-related problems included:

- blue-screen incidents;
- poor performance;
- dust accumulation;
- connectivity degradation; and
- other hardware conditions capable of generating repeated support demand.

The relevant problem category generated approximately **15 calls per day**.

The recurring pattern was predominantly reactive.

Problems emerged, users reported them, IT responded, and the cycle repeated.

## 6.2 Intervention

A **quarterly preventive hardware health check** was introduced.

The intervention shifted the operating logic from repeated response toward preventive condition management.

The primary 5R logic was:

> **RE-ENGINEER**

## 6.3 Observed Result

Related recurring calls were reported to have been reduced to **zero following the intervention** (author-reported; the observation period and records are Not recorded).

The intervention therefore changed the conditions generating the demand rather than merely improving the response to incidents.

## 6.4 HEE Interpretation

The case illustrates the movement:

> **Response → Prevention**

The relevant HEE question becomes:

> **Why should recurring incidents continue to consume organizational capacity if their underlying conditions can be addressed beforehand?**

Prevention can preserve more capacity than repeated response when recurring problems originate from addressable operating conditions.

# 7. CASE 4 — REPORT PERFORMANCE AND INFORMATION RICHNESS

## 7.1 Operating Condition

A recurring report experienced two related problems.

First, report generation was unnecessarily slow.

Second, the information produced was insufficiently rich and contextual for effective user application.

The report required approximately **9 minutes** to produce.

The problem was therefore not simply one of technical speed.

It involved the relationship between:

- report structure;
- query and system performance;
- information richness;
- contextual usefulness; and
- system resource consumption.

## 7.2 Intervention

The report was re-engineered across these dimensions.

The intervention addressed:

- report structure;
- query performance;
- system performance;
- information richness;
- contextual usefulness; and
- system health.

The underlying principle was:

> **Question before optimizing.**

Rather than merely trying to make the existing report run faster, the report and its surrounding conditions were reconsidered.

## 7.3 Observed Result

Report-generation time changed from approximately:

> **9 minutes → 9 seconds**

CPU, RAM, and I/O utilization were brought below the applicable operating threshold, while information usefulness was improved.

### Measurement Basis

The before-and-after figures represent observed report-generation time under the respective pre- and post-redesign configurations. They are presented as an **operating observation rather than a controlled benchmark study**.

## 7.4 HEE Interpretation

The significance of this case is not simply the speed improvement.

It illustrates that performance improvement can involve both:

1. **process efficiency**, and
2. **information design**.

The intervention changed the relationship between demand, information, system resources, and user application.

This illustrates a broader HEE question:

> **What is the organization actually consuming to produce the required information, and can the entire relationship be redesigned?**

# 8. CASE 5 — CAPABILITY ENABLEMENT: INTERNAL APPLICATION WITHOUT EXTERNAL VENDOR COST

## 8.1 Operating Condition

Internal technical capability existed, or could be developed, for relevant infrastructure work.

However, organizational conditions had created a pattern of external dependency.

The issue was therefore not simply whether the organization possessed technical knowledge.

The deeper question was whether existing capability could be converted into effective organizational application.

This produced an important HEE distinction:

> **Capability is potential. Capacity is the part of it available in time, attention, and energy. Enablement is whether it can be reached. Application puts it to work.**

Capability alone does not constitute available organizational capacity.

## 8.2 Intervention

The role of an Infrastructure Engineer was expanded beyond narrow technical execution.

The operating scope included:

- understanding problems;
- designing solutions;
- implementing solutions;
- conducting health checks;
- contributing to architecture;
- supporting continuous improvement; and
- taking greater ownership of infrastructure outcomes.

The intervention therefore went beyond training.

It addressed the organizational conditions required for capability to become application.

## 8.3 Observed Result

Applicable infrastructure work was undertaken internally without external vendor cost. The vendor cost avoided and the internal time spent are **Not recorded** in this edition, so the size of the saving is Unknown.

The important change was therefore not simply the acquisition of additional knowledge.

It was the conversion of internal capability into effective application.

The logic can be represented as:

**Capability → Application → Execution**

rather than:

**Capability → Dependency → External Consumption**

## 8.4 HEE Interpretation

This case became particularly important to the development of **Human Energy Enablement (HEEn)**.

It illustrated that an organization may possess capability while still failing to obtain the capacity that capability could provide.

The constraint may lie in organizational conditions rather than in the capability itself.

# 9. CASE 6 — SOLUTION PURCHASE AND MONTH-WISE STOCKING

## 9.1 Operating Condition

Small devices and accessories were repeatedly required in predictable patterns.

However, these requirements were processed as individual procurement events.

The resulting process involved repeated:

- requests;
- approvals;
- procurement activity;
- coordination; and
- fulfillment.

The underlying demand was legitimate.

The problem was the way predictable demand was being processed.

## 9.2 Intervention

The procurement approach was redesigned through:

- functional solution purchasing; and
- month-wise stocking.

The relevant 5R logic was:

> **RE-ENGINEER + RETAIN**

The intervention did not seek to eliminate legitimate demand.

It redesigned how that demand was satisfied.

## 9.3 Observed Result

The redesigned approach produced:

- faster fulfillment; and
- reduced recurring process man-hours.

The organization continued to satisfy legitimate requirements while reducing the recurring process consumption associated with satisfying them individually.

## 9.4 HEE Interpretation

This case illustrates why **RETAIN** is an essential part of the 5R Cascade.

Not all recurring demand is unnecessary.

Some demand creates legitimate value and should remain.

The HEE question is therefore not:

> **How do we eliminate recurring demand?**

It is:

> **Which recurring demand should be eliminated, which should be reduced or replaced, and which should be retained but satisfied differently?**

This distinction prevents demand reduction from becoming an objective in itself.

# 11. WHAT THE CASES REVEAL ABOUT ORGANIZATIONAL DRAIN

The cases suggest that organizational drain can arise through many mechanisms.

Human Energy and organizational capacity can be consumed through:

- support calls;
- interruptions;
- reactive work;
- service chains;
- preventable incidents;
- procurement activity;
- coordination;
- external dependency;
- repeated decisions;
- waiting;
- handoffs;
- recurring activities; and
- work that could be redesigned, prevented, replaced, or removed.

This means organizational drain is not simply a function of workload volume.

Drain can also arise from the **structure, recurrence, dependency, and conditions surrounding demand**.

A recurring pattern can therefore be expressed as:

**Operational Demand → Human Energy Consumption → Capacity Reduction**

The intervention seeks to reverse the relationship:

**Diagnose Condition → Change Condition → Reduce Consumption → Preserve Capacity**

This is one of the central practical observations underlying HEE.

# 12. HEE ARCHITECTURE OBSERVED IN PRACTICE

The cases provide practical illustrations of several HEE constructs.

## 12.1 Human Energy

Human Energy is consumed through:

- support calls;
- interruptions;
- reactive work;
- coordination;
- preventable incidents;
- procurement;
- decisions;
- waiting;
- handoffs;
- external dependency; and
- repetitive recurring activity.

The relevant capacity question is not merely how much work exists, but how much Human Energy is consumed to sustain it.

## 12.2 Capability

Capability represents what people or organizations know and can potentially do.

But:

> **Capability is potential.**

Capability does not automatically become available organizational capacity.

## 12.3 Capacity

Capacity is the part of capability available in time, attention, and energy. Enablement determines whether it can be reached, and application puts it to work.

This distinction is particularly visible in the capability-enablement case.

## 12.4 Connection

Connection concerns the relationships among:

- people;
- functions;
- systems;
- information;
- requirements; and
- decisions.

Disconnects create additional consumption.

A technically simple task can therefore generate substantial organizational drain if the surrounding connections are poorly designed.

## 12.5 Enablement

Enablement concerns the organizational conditions that allow Human Energy and capability to become effective application.

Where capability exists but application is constrained, the issue is not necessarily a capability gap.

It may be an enablement gap.

## 12.6 Demand

Demand is not inherently negative.

HEE focuses particularly on **unnecessary or unnecessarily consuming recurring demand**.

The objective is not to eliminate valuable work.

It is to distinguish between:

- demand that should disappear;
- demand that should be reduced;
- demand that can be replaced;
- demand whose conditions should be re-engineered; and
- demand that should legitimately remain.

## 12.7 3C Strategic Lens

The cases also reflect the **3C Strategic Lens**:

**SOURCE → DEPENDENCY → CONSTRAINT**

The purpose is to identify what generates recurring consumption, what dependency sustains it, and what condition prevents a better operating state.

## 12.8 5R Cascade

The intervention logic can then be applied:

**REMOVE → REDUCE → REPLACE → RE-ENGINEER → RETAIN**

The cases suggest that different operating conditions may call for different interventions.

## 12.9 Human Energy Enablement

Where capability exists but cannot be effectively applied, **HEEn** becomes relevant.

Its practical logic is:

**Capability → Application → Execution**

## 12.10 Execution Excellence

Preserved capacity ultimately needs to be connected to organizational priorities.

This is where the broader execution logic of HEE and the **Execution Excellence System (EES)** becomes relevant.

Preserving capacity without directing it toward valuable application does not automatically create organizational value.

# 13. FROM DEMAND REDUCTION TO CAPACITY PRESERVATION

Demand reduction is not the final objective.

The deeper sequence is:

**Demand Reduction → Human Energy No Longer Consumed → Capacity Preserved → Capacity Available for Application**

This distinction matters because released capacity can simply be reabsorbed by other low-value work.

Stopping the drain therefore creates an opportunity.

It does not automatically create value.

The preserved capacity must be protected and deliberately redirected toward higher-value application, such as:

- capability development;
- innovation;
- strategy;
- customer value;
- organizational improvement;
- problem prevention;
- higher-value execution; and
- growth.

Therefore:

> **Stopping organizational drain creates capacity opportunity; deliberate application converts that opportunity into value.**

The cases in this record do not document where released capacity went or who owned it. The Core README's reabsorption prediction (P13) is proposed and untested, and this record neither supports nor tests it: the destination of released capacity is **Unknown** for every case.

# 15. EVOLUTION OF THE OPERATING LOGIC

Across the cases, the operating logic evolved through several stages:

**Request Processing → Problem Elimination → System Improvement → Capability Development → Solution Creation → Organizational Enablement**

The shift was significant.

The function moved progressively from asking:

> **How can IT handle requests better?**

toward:

> **How can the organization require fewer requests?**

and ultimately toward:

> **How can preserved capacity be applied to more valuable work?**

This represents a shift from responding to demand toward changing the conditions that generate demand.

# 16. COMMON LOGIC ACROSS THE CASES

Although the six cases differ in subject matter, they share a common operating sequence.

### Step 1 — Identify Drain

Determine where recurring Human Energy and organizational capacity are being consumed.

### Step 2 — Diagnose Source

Understand the condition generating or sustaining the demand.

### Step 3 — Select Intervention

Apply the appropriate intervention:

**REMOVE → REDUCE → REPLACE → RE-ENGINEER → RETAIN**

### Step 4 — Stop or Reduce Drain

Change the condition so that unnecessary consumption decreases or disappears.

### Step 5 — Preserve Capacity

Ensure that the Human Energy and organizational capacity no longer consumed by the previous activity remain available.

### Step 6 — Enable Application

Create the conditions for preserved capacity and existing capability to become useful application.

### Step 7 — Strengthen Execution

Direct the resulting capacity toward higher-value organizational priorities.

The logic therefore goes beyond workload reduction.

It is:

> **Drain reduction → Capacity preservation → Productive application → Stronger execution**

# 17. CENTRAL LESSON

The conventional management question is often:

> **How can we make people handle more?**

HEE introduces a different question:

> **What should we stop making people handle?**

This changes the management sequence.

Instead of continuously increasing the organization's ability to absorb demand:

**Identify unnecessary demand → Stop the drain → Preserve Human Energy → Protect capacity → Enable capability → Apply capacity to higher-value work → Strengthen execution**

The central practical lesson is:

> **Stopping the drain is the beginning of capacity preservation, not the end of the intervention.**

# 18. FROM OPERATING RECORD TO RESEARCH PROGRAM

The AKG record represents operating evidence and observation that contributed to the development of HEE.

It should not be treated as controlled empirical proof.

The appropriate progression is:

**Operating Evidence → Observation → Proposition → Theory Development → Further Research**

The cases raise questions such as:

- How frequently does recurring organizational drain occur?
- Which forms of demand consume the greatest amount of Human Energy?
- Which intervention types are most effective under which conditions?
- What organizational conditions determine whether capability becomes capacity?
- How should preserved capacity be measured?
- What is the relationship between preserved capacity and execution quality?
- How does Human Energy interact with organizational capacity, employee experience, and customer experience?
- Under what conditions are these interventions replicable?
- What are the longitudinal effects of stopping recurring organizational drain?

Further development therefore requires:

- additional cases;
- independent application and measurement;
- longitudinal observation;
- comparative studies;
- construct development and measurement; and
- replication across different organizational contexts.

The broader progression is:

**Theory → Application → Evidence → Research**

They constitute:

> **documented operating evidence and proposition-generating observations, not universal proof.**

This distinction is deliberately maintained because the credibility of a developing theory depends not only on what it claims, but also on what it refuses to claim.

**Request Processing → Problem Elimination → System Improvement → Capability Development → Solution Creation → Organizational Enablement**

The reported changes did not come from a single mechanism.

It came from changing the relationship between recurring organizational demand and available capacity.

This included:

- reducing unnecessary requests;
- preventing recurring problems;
- enabling users through self-service;
- developing internal capability;
- reducing external dependency;
- redesigning recurring processes;
- improving information and decision support;

> **How much unnecessary work it could prevent the organization from having to absorb.**

That was a significant change in operating perspective.

# 21. CENTRAL HEE INSIGHT

> **Organizations can lose capacity without losing people.**

People may remain.

Headcount may remain.

Capability may remain.

Yet organizational capacity can decline because Human Energy is continuously consumed by:

- recurring demand;
- operational noise;
- coordination;
- preventable problems;
- dependencies;
- unnecessary activity; and
- obsolete or poorly designed work.

The HEE question is therefore:

> **What is consuming organizational capacity unnecessarily?**

The 5R Cascade provides a disposition logic:

**REMOVE → REDUCE → REPLACE → RE-ENGINEER → RETAIN**

Where capability exists but application is constrained, **HEEn** addresses the organizational conditions preventing effective application.

Preserved capacity can then be aligned to execution through **EES**.

The resulting architecture is:

**HEE → OSF → 3C + 5R → HEEn → EES → Sustainable Execution Capacity → Sustainable Business Value**

# 22. CONCLUSION

The AKG cases suggest that recurring organizational demand can consume Human Energy and organizational capacity long after the original reason for that consumption has ceased to justify it.

The interventions explored different ways of changing that condition:

- eliminating unnecessary demand;
- replacing human-mediated work;
- preventing recurring problems;
- redesigning processes;
- improving information and system design;
- developing and enabling internal capability;
- reducing unnecessary dependency; and
- redesigning predictable recurring demand.

The central question is therefore not simply:

> **How can we improve the work?**

It is:

> **Should the organization still be doing this work in this form?**

The practical HEE logic is:

**IDENTIFY DRAIN → DIAGNOSE CONDITION → SELECT INTERVENTION → STOP / REDUCE DRAIN → PRESERVE CAPACITY → ENABLE APPLICATION → STRENGTHEN EXECUTION**

The objective is not simply less work.

The objective is **more organizational capacity available for valuable work**.

The distinction between capability and capacity remains central:

> **Capability is potential. Capacity is the part of it available in time, attention, and energy. Application puts it to work.**

And the broader principle remains:

> **Extraction depletes. Cultivation compounds.**

HEE did not begin as a theory imposed upon these cases.

The theory emerged through repeated operating observations.

Observations became interventions.
